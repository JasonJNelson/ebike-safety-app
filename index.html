E-Bike Classes Explained

Class 1

Assist up to 20 mph

Pedal-assist only. Motor helps only while you pedal. Widest trail & path access.

Class 2

Assist up to 20 mph

Throttle + pedal-assist. Motor can propel without pedaling. Some path restrictions.

Class 3

Assist up to 28 mph

Pedal-assist only (higher speed). Often road/bike-lane only; helmet & age rules common.

Federal baseline (15 U.S.C. §2085): low-speed electric bicycle ≤750 W, operable pedals, motor-only ≤20 mph. States define classes and riding rules. Oregon allows up to 1,000 W in some cases. New York Class 3 is capped at 25 mph.

Park & Street Rules (General)

  • Public Streets & Bike Lanes
    Most states treat Class 1–3 e-bikes like bicycles on roads and in bike lanes. Follow all traffic laws (stop signs, signals, yield). Class 3 may be restricted from some shared paths.
  • Sidewalks
    Generally prohibited or heavily restricted in cities. Assume sidewalks are off-limits unless local law explicitly allows e-bikes.
  • Multi-Use Paths & Trails
    Class 1 usually has the broadest access. Class 2 (throttle) and Class 3 face more restrictions. Local managers and cities can ban specific classes.
  • National Parks (NPS)
    E-bikes (all three classes) allowed where traditional bicycles are permitted — typically roads and designated bike paths, not hiking-only trails or wilderness. Check each park’s superintendent’s compendium.
  • Federal Lands (BLM / Forest Service)
    BLM generally allows Class 1–3 where bikes are allowed. U.S. Forest Service often treats e-bikes as motorized on non-motorized trails — check the local ranger district.
  • Local Override
    Cities, counties, and park agencies can impose stricter rules (e.g., NYC 15 mph cap, Denver park policies). Always check posted signs and local ordinances.
